Describe an incident you handled and what you changed afterward.

1 evidence

A failed database failover led to a postmortem and a runbook still in use today.

A primary database failed over during a deploy window and the replica came up read only, so every write in the product failed for eleven minutes. I was on call, rolled the deploy back, and promoted the replica by hand1. Afterwards I ran the postmortem and wrote the failover runbook that the team still uses, with the manual promotion steps and the checks that would have caught the read-only state in the first minute.

What kind of team and manager do you work best with?

Async first, written decisions, and a manager who sets direction and gets out of the way.

I do my best work on teams that write things down. Decisions in a doc with the options that were rejected, a weekly written update instead of a status meeting, and a manager who sets the direction clearly and then trusts the team to get there. I have worked in the other mode, and I can, but I am slower and less honest in it.

Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision.

Argued against a rewrite, lost, and then made the rewrite succeed.

Two years ago the team decided to rewrite the job scheduler rather than fix it. I argued for the fix in the design review, with numbers on the risk, and lost the vote. Once it was decided I took the hardest part of the rewrite, the migration of running jobs, so the thing I was most worried about was the thing I owned. It shipped late but without an incident, and I would make the same call about how to disagree again.
